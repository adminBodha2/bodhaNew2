# Pramana — A Dharmic Field Library

*Cognitive and communicative concepts from the Sanskrit intellectual traditions, for designers, researchers, and people building and leading with AI.*

A reference of operational concepts drawn from Nyaya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta, Vyakarana, and Alankara-shastra, plus a small set of concepts from outside the six major schools. Each card names a move you can make in your work — a question you can ask, a discrimination you can draw — that the available English vocabulary does not give you a clean way to name.

A concept earns its card only when it gives you something the existing English vocabulary does not. Where a Sanskrit term only renames a concept already serviceable in English, it has been kept out.

---

## How to read a card

Each card has an English title leading, with the Sanskrit term as a subtitle. The card carries a tradition ID (N for Nyaya, Y for Yoga, M for Mimamsa, V for Vedanta, G for Vyakarana grammar, A for Alankara-shastra poetics, O for other), an applicability tag — **Users** of AI tools, **Builders** of AI products, **Stewards** who set direction — a gist line, then a body in plain English. Each card closes with a diagnostic question, an **Indic roots** section for the Sanskrit apparatus, and a short further-reading note.

A **Where the closest English-language analog falls short** section appears where the comparison sharpens the card. Some cards skip it because the concept has no obvious English near-neighbour to set against.

## Applicability tags

- **Users** — anyone working with AI tools day-to-day. Writers using LLMs, knowledge workers querying assistants, anyone receiving AI output as part of their work.
- **Builders** — anyone making AI products. Developers, designers, prompt engineers, ML practitioners, UX writers, anyone touching how the product behaves.
- **Stewards** — anyone setting direction or judging the work. Product managers, design leads, research leads, founders, evaluators, brand owners, executives.

A card may apply to one, two, or three of these. The tag on each card tells you where the operational lever lives most cleanly.
